## Newton's Four Core Concepts
- Velocity

## Newton's Laws (as introduced)
- Inertia — bodies with no external influence/force are unaccelerated
- Falling
- Force pairs
- Gravity
- **Naming note**: the textbook deliberately avoids "Newton's first/second/third law" as vague and uses descriptive names instead — Law of Inertia = "1st law" (though it originated with Descartes, see Module 3), **Newton's Law of Motion** (a ∝ F/m, Section 3 below) = "2nd law," **Law of Force Pairs** = "3rd law"

## Force
- **Force** = any external influence that causes a body to accelerate
- "Accelerate" doesn't just mean speed up — it can also mean slow down or change direction
- **Force pairs (Newton's Law of Force Pairs)**: whenever one body exerts a force on a second body, the second exerts a force back on the first — and **the two forces are always exactly equal in strength and opposite in direction**, with no exceptions (this is the textbook's actual definition — worth memorizing precisely)
  - Example: you standing on the ground — you push down on it, it pushes back up on you, equally
  - **CORRECTED — Quicksand is *not* a force-pairs example.** The textbook's Law of Force Pairs says these paired forces are *always* equal — there's no "unequal force pair." What's actually happening with quicksand is a **net force** imbalance between two *different* forces: gravity pulling you down vs. the *separate* support/normal force quicksand can push back with. When your weight exceeds the max support force quicksand supplies, you sink — but that's a net-force story, not a force-pairs one
- **Friction** = the force of one surface acting against another
- **Air resistance** = the force caused by hitting air molecules during movement
- **Gravitational force** = a pull that causes objects to always accelerate downward, toward Earth's center

## Force, Mass, and Acceleration
- Forces cause accelerations
- An object's acceleration is proportional to the amount of force applied to it
- **Inertia** = a body's resistance to acceleration (colloquially, its "weight")
- **Mass** = the amount of inertia an object has
- An object's acceleration is proportional to the force exerted on it, divided by its mass:
  - **a = F / m**

## Net Force
- **Net force** = when forces act in the *same* direction, their effects add; when they act in *opposite* directions, the net force is their difference, in the direction of the larger force
- Pushing a book across a table with **10 N** of force while the tabletop's **friction** exerts **3 N** back on it → net force = **7 N**. Both forces act along the same horizontal line, which is why they simply subtract.

## Key Terms
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Force | External influence that causes a body to accelerate |
| Friction | Force of one surface against another |
| Air resistance | Force from an object's collisions with air molecules |
| Gravitational force | Pull causing acceleration toward Earth's center |
| Inertia | A body's resistance to acceleration |
| Mass | The amount of inertia an object has |
| Net force | The combined effect of multiple forces on a body |

## Formula
